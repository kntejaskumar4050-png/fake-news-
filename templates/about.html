About the project

A deep learning approach to
news credibility signals.

Fake news spreads faster than corrections. This final-year engineering project explores whether recurrent neural networks can learn the stylistic fingerprints that separate fabricated articles from professional reporting — and how honestly those predictions can be presented.

Project typeDeep Learning · NLP
DatasetKaggle Fake & Real News
ArchitecturesLSTM · BiLSTM
DeploymentFlask + SQLite
Problem statement

Why build this?

Misinformation is cheap to produce and expensive to debunk. Manual fact-checking does not scale to the volume of content published every hour. At the same time, fabricated articles often share detectable stylistic traits: sensational claims, unusual vocabulary, repetitive phrasing and structural differences from wire-service reporting.

This project tests the hypothesis that a sequence model can learn those traits from labelled data. It is deliberately framed as a pattern detector, not an arbiter of truth: the goal is to build, evaluate and explain an end-to-end machine learning system, and to communicate its limits as clearly as its results.

Objectives

What the system delivers

  • Clean and deduplicate a real labelled news corpus.
  • Build a leakage-free preprocessing and tokenisation pipeline.
  • Train LSTM and BiLSTM classifiers with regularisation and early stopping.
  • Evaluate both models with accuracy, precision, recall, F1, ROC-AUC and confusion matrices.
  • Serve real-time predictions through a documented Flask API.
  • Persist predictions in SQLite and visualise usage statistics.
  • Present confidence responsibly, with explicit reliability cues.
Methodology

How the models were built

A reproducible pipeline from raw CSV files to deployed inference artifacts.

  1. 01

    Dataset assembly

    Fake.csv and True.csv are loaded with Pandas, labelled (0 = fake, 1 = real), combined and shuffled with a fixed seed. Missing rows, exact duplicates and unusable short texts are removed.

  2. 02

    Leakage-free preprocessing

    Title and body are merged, HTML and URLs stripped, unicode normalised, lowercased, stripped of punctuation and digits, tokenised and filtered with NLTK stopwords (negations retained). Cleaning is stateless, so it can run before splitting. The dataset is split 70 / 15 / 15 — stratified — and the Keras Tokenizer is fitted only on the training split.

  3. 03

    Sequence encoding

    Each article becomes a sequence of integer token IDs capped at {{ dataset_info.get('max_len', 256) }} tokens, padded post-sequence. The embedding layer learns a 128-dimensional vector per token during training.

  4. 04

    Model training

    Both models use the same embedding, dropout and dense head. The LSTM reads left-to-right; the BiLSTM reads in both directions. Training uses Adam, binary cross-entropy, batch size 128, early stopping on validation loss (patience 3), learning-rate reduction and best-checkpoint restore.

  5. 05

    Evaluation & deployment

    Both checkpoints are scored once on the untouched test split. Metrics, confusion matrices, training curves and the comparison table are written to models/metrics.json and static/images/. The best model is deployed behind the Flask API, with the tokenizer and preprocessing configuration saved alongside it.

Limitations & ethics

What this model cannot tell you

Academic honesty matters more than an impressive demo. These are real constraints, not fine print.

It is not a fact-checker

The output is a classification of writing style learned from one corpus. A high confidence score means the text resembles one class in that corpus — not that its claims are true.

Source and topic bias

Fake articles in the dataset come largely from a set of alternative outlets and real articles largely from Reuters. The model can learn publisher-specific cues and may not generalise to new domains.

English & length dependent

Training data is English-only and truncated at 256 tokens. Very short inputs, satire, translations and mixed-format content fall outside the model's competence.

Dataset staleness

The corpus covers 2015–2018. Vocabulary and writing conventions shift, so performance on modern content should be re-validated, not assumed.

No causal explanation

Confidence and per-model agreement are provided, but the network does not cite evidence. Advisory output must never be treated as a verdict on a publisher.

Misuse risk

Automated labels can amplify error at scale. The interface intentionally frames results as signals, surfaces reliability warnings and stores no personal data.
